# Bike Purchase Behaviour Analysis

## Overview

This project uses Microsoft Excel to explore customer characteristics and bike purchasing behaviour. The analysis examines purchasing patterns across age, income, region, commute distance, and vehicle ownership, presenting the findings in an interactive dashboard.

The objective is to demonstrate how spreadsheet-based data preparation and analysis can support customer segmentation and inform business decisions.

## Business Question

**How can customer demographic and commuting data be used to identify bike purchasing patterns and inform marketing strategies?**

## Tools Used

- Microsoft Excel
- Excel formulas and calculated columns
- PivotTables and PivotCharts
- Interactive dashboard slicers

## Project Workflow

1. Created a separate working sheet to preserve the original dataset.
2. Removed duplicate records from the working dataset.
3. Standardised marital status values (`S` and `M`) to `Single` and `Married`, respectively, and gender values (`F` and `M`) to `Female` and `Male`.
4. Created age categories: Young Adults (30 and below), Middle-Aged (31–59), and Seniors (60 and above).
5. Created income categories: Low Income (below $40,000), Moderate Income ($40,000–$80,000), and High Income (above $80,000).
6. Used PivotTables and PivotCharts to analyse purchasing patterns across customer attributes.
7. Developed an interactive dashboard with summary metrics, visualisations, and slicers.

## Dashboard

The dashboard presents the following analyses:

- Buyer and non-buyer distribution
- Purchase patterns by age and income
- Regional customer distribution
- Commute distance across age groups
- Bike buyer distribution by commute distance and vehicle ownership

Interactive slicers allow users to explore the data by marital status, bike purchase status, gender, and region.

## Key Findings

- **Purchase distribution:** Of 1,000 customer records, 481 were bike buyers (48.1%) and 519 were non-buyers (51.9%).
- **Income differences:** Average income was $57,963 among buyers and $54,875 among non-buyers.
- **Age differences:** Average age was 43 among buyers and 45 among non-buyers.
- **Age and income segment:** Middle-aged customers with moderate incomes accounted for 53.01% of all bike buyers.
- **Regional distribution:** North America accounted for 45.74% of all bike buyers, Europe 30.77%, and the Pacific region 23.49%.
- **Regional purchase rate:** The Pacific region had a reported purchase rate of approximately 57.9%.
- **Vehicle ownership:** Customers owning zero or one car accounted for approximately 62.99% of all bike buyers.

These findings describe patterns within the dataset and do not establish causation. The share of all buyers represented by a segment is different from the purchase rate within that segment.

## Business Recommendations

- Explore marketing strategies for short-distance commuters and compare purchase rates across commute categories.
- Investigate the middle-aged, moderate-income segment and compare its purchase rate with other customer groups.
- Assess whether customers owning zero or one car have higher purchase rates than customers with more cars.
- Consider both buyer distribution and regional purchase rates when evaluating regional marketing opportunities.
- Use demographic and behavioural attributes to develop customer segments and validate potential opportunities through further analysis.

## Limitations

- The analysis identifies patterns and associations rather than proving causal relationships.
- Buyer distribution and purchase rate measure different things and should not be used interchangeably.
- The findings reflect the available dataset and may not generalise to other customer populations.
- The recommendations are proposed opportunities for further investigation, not tested business outcomes.
